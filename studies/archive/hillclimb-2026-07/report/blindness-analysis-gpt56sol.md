# Why Playtest missed the faults—and what “release oracle” requires

## Decision

Playtest missed faults mainly because the suite did not force the exact state and action that exposed them, not because the browser could not see them or because `gpt-5.4-mini` was simply too weak. Of the 12 faults never surfaced, I classify **8 as primarily deterministic-coverage/precondition failures, 3 as reached-surface actor-recognition failures, and 1 as a structural oracle failure**. The grader was a material second line of failure on two reached cases, but no miss is convincingly grader-only. Vision is ruled out for this set.

This means the highest-return change is not “use a smarter persona.” Convert risk cases into state-controlled journeys with mandatory actions and machine-checkable postconditions. Keep LLM actors for exploration and qualitative harm; put business truth and transition correctness in assertions. Without that hybrid, “two clean rounds” means only “this stochastic panel generated no new accepted reports,” not “safe to release.” The observed ceiling was 14/26, or 53.8%, even though the policy fixer repaired 20/26, including six faults identified through code/class generalization rather than Playtest detection (`studies/hillclimb/report/WRITEUP.md`, fault accounting at lines 232–252).

## Root-cause assignment

The actor entered `riley.chen@fastmail.example` and saved; it never submitted an invalid email, so the swallowed server rejection could not fire (`runs/2026-07-10T1134-4567/moving-house@returning-regular/trajectory.jsonl`, steps 3–4). | Submit a known-invalid email; assert visible error, retained input, and unchanged persisted value. |

| `f-clear-search-self-link` | Coverage/masking | Same missing precondition, plus no required click on “Clear the search.” It was fixed by policy class generalization, not observed. | From the guaranteed empty state, click the named link; assert query removal, non-empty catalog, and URL change. |
| `f-continue-shopping-loop` | Coverage; actor stops at goal | The receipt surface was reached, but the story ended when purchase/status was proven. In the receipt run, the snapshot exposed `Continue shopping`; the actor immediately returned `done`, so the faulty link was never exercised (`runs/2026-07-11T1255-1ae9/wheres-my-order@returning-regular/trajectory.jsonl`, step 4). | After every terminal state, require each primary recovery CTA once; assert destination semantics, not merely navigation success. |

| `f-sort-inert` | Actor false verification; grader second | The actor selected low-to-high and predicted the cheapest item would move first. Prompt actor and grader to reconcile every explicit expectation with the next observation. |
| `f-oos-says-in-stock` | Actor semantic cross-check failure; grader second | This is the cleanest perception failure. Step 4 contains both accessible strings: “In stock — more arriving daily” and disabled “Out of stock.” The actor focused on the disabled button, called the item unavailable, and navigated away (`runs/2026-07-11T1456-9d4f/first-impressions@cautious-first-timer/trajectory.jsonl`, step 4). Grader must independently compare co-located claims, not merely summarize actor concern. |
| `f-receipt-eta-wrong` | **Oracle gap**; semantic checking second | The actor accurately read “Order … is on its way” and “confirmed · arrives in 3–5 days,” then quoted the ETA as fact and ended (`runs/2026-07-11T1255-1ae9/wheres-my-order@returning-regular/trajectory.jsonl`, step 4). Acting cannot know the status-to-copy rule. | A spec/data oracle must assert the copy by status: confirmed, shipped, delivered. An LLM may judge clarity after the value is verified, but must not invent ground truth. |

The ratchet re-injection of `f-validation-wipes-payment` reinforces the first category. Its supposedly pinned `checkout-recovery` guard passed green at score 94 because the actor used valid data; the ledger answer says validation “was not exercised” (`studies/hillclimb/ledger/policy/round-01.json`, line 2135; `studies/hillclimb/report/WRITEUP.md`, lines 283–296). A story naming a behavior in its report questions is not coverage. Only an executed precondition plus observed postcondition is coverage.

## Which lever dominates

**By missed-fault count, deterministic coverage dominates: 8/12.** But this does not justify adding more broad narratives or repeats. The two baseline rounds rediscovered the same 13 faults and added none. Coverage must be represented as `(seeded state, mandatory action, expected transition)`, with the harness recording whether each obligation executed. Repeats help only after that probability is non-zero.

**Actor prompting/persona design addresses about 3/12 primary misses** (`sort`, OOS copy, cart continuation) and improves secondary detection of the receipt contradiction. The current personas encourage goal completion and route flexibility; that is realistic user simulation but the wrong objective for release gating. Add separate modes rather than corrupting personas into universal QA bots: normal-user discovery, adversarial error/boundary testing, and semantic-consistency audit.

**At least 1/12 is irreducibly oracle-bound** (`f-receipt-eta-wrong`), and more production faults will share that property: tax, entitlement, inventory, dates, eligibility, localization, and policy values can all be plausible but wrong. This is the hard ceiling of journey-only testing. The grader cannot recover truth absent a contract. The evidence supports grader changes for `sort` and OOS, but a higher grader tier is not the leading bet: `gpt-5.5` already had the relevant snapshots, and no actor/grader tier factorial was run.

Vision is not causal. Vision and axe were active; axe caught both contrast faults. More decisively, the named misses appear verbatim in `snapshot_text`, including button disabled state, item order, links, and ETA. The failure was interpreting or exercising visible information. Better pixels do not provide status rules or force an invalid submission.

## Prioritized roadmap

1. **Build a deterministic risk matrix and make it the release gate.** For every critical route, enumerate happy path, validation failure, retry/retention, exact boundary, empty state, and recovery CTA. Seed server state explicitly. Fail a run as “not covered” if any required action was skipped. Start with the 12 guards in the table; this would make 8 current misses directly testable and make the three comparison misses machine-verifiable.

2. **Add contract oracles beside journeys.** Assert status-to-copy mappings, inventory/cart arithmetic, totals, redirect destinations, required page-local affordances, and persisted data. Use DOM/API assertions where exact truth exists. The release decision should be `deterministic contracts green AND required transitions covered AND no unresolved high-severity LLM findings`, never an LLM cleanliness score alone.

3. **Make the actor switch modes.** Keep personas for realistic discovery. Add an adversarial pass instructed to enter one invalid value per form, test formatted/unformatted equivalents, exercise `0/1/N/N+1`, retry after errors, click terminal/recovery controls, and compare before/after state. Give it a checklist of obligations, not expected fault answers, to reduce leakage while preventing improvisational skips.

4. **Turn semantic comparison into a first-class grader job.** Independently compare the actor’s expectation with the next snapshot; reconcile labels with disabled/enabled states; compare totals and status claims; flag “worked” claims unsupported by a changed result. Require a structured verdict per obligation: executed, observed delta, oracle source, pass/fail/unknown. “Unknown” must not become green.

5. **Change stop and reporting semantics.** Report detection recall, deterministic obligation coverage, assertion pass rate, repair-by-inspection, and live residuals separately. “Two clean rounds” may stop exploration spend, but release requires the deterministic gate. Deduplicate the roughly 190-report false-positive floor before review so prominence and below-fold noise do not crowd out contradictions.

Model escalation ranks last. Use a stronger actor only for a named ambiguity after the forced suite exists; this study contains no evidence that a default upgrade would close the observed gaps.

## Would a human still have a poor experience after “clean”?

**Yes.** The standard in-stock, ordinary-quantity purchase trunk was substantially repaired, so not every shopper would fail. But “clean” left repeatable defects on common post-purchase and recovery paths. Both arms retained the blatant OOS stock contradiction, wrong shipped-order ETA, receipt continuation loop, and missing cart-local continuation. A real shopper checking an unavailable product would distrust the catalog; a returning customer checking a late shipment would receive misleading status; a buyer continuing after purchase would be dumped into an empty-cart loop; a cart shopper would have to notice the global nav.

The policy-clean app also retained the silent invalid-email failure and account-form reset: edge/error paths, but realistic and destructive to confidence. The naive-clean app retained nine missed faults plus the accepted missing product breadcrumb, including the major last-unit purchase failure, an empty-cart checkout form charging $6 for nothing, broken no-results recovery, and developer jargon. Thus the core happy path was mostly healthy; boundaries, invalid input, empty states, secondary navigation, and post-purchase truth were not. Users on those paths would still hit bugs and, in several cases, have a poor experience.

## Where this read differs

The brief and prior write-ups group wrong ETA with “misleading copy” and recommend another forced compare journey. I disagree with that as the primary remedy: forcing the receipt open already happened repeatedly; without a status/content oracle, the ETA remains plausible and the actor has no basis to reject it. I also would not summarize the result as “L3 is inherently hard and L4 easy.” The data show **uncued transitions are hard and goal-cued absences are easy**: the missed L4 link was optional and routable, while detected L4 capabilities were central to explicit goals. Finally, I assign more blame to suite architecture than the phrase “AI user-tester missed” implies. The actor did fail conspicuously on OOS and sort, and the grader failed to rescue both, but two-thirds of the 12 misses were never deterministically activated. The release-oracle gap is therefore primarily an instrumentation/specification problem, not a vision problem and not yet evidence for a model-upgrade decision.
