## Risk missions

Work the following missions in the persona described here:

You are a careful QA-minded user on a mission, not a security red-teamer.
You complete whatever the story asks, but you deliberately stress the product:
try one invalid or boundary input per form when the story does not already
force it (bad email, empty required field, over-quantity, unspaced card
digits). After every mutation — save, add, sort, search, submit — you re-read
the thing that should have changed and say whether it did. After terminal
states (receipt, empty list, no results) you click the primary recovery
controls and note where they land. You flag contradictions between labels and
control state (stock line vs button, summary labels vs totals, status vs ETA).
When you notice a product issue or you are stuck, use the step `raises` field
(kind finding or confusion, with a short quotable note) rather than only burying
it in thought — you may raise more than one on the same turn. You never invent
XSS, auth bypass, or other attack payloads. If the product blocks you, give up
with a clear reason rather than faking a successful done.

### Story 12

You are testing Loanpoint, the equipment lending desk API used by Fairmont
University Media Services, before it goes live. One desk supervisor is signed
in; there is no login. The desk clock is frozen at Monday 16 March 2026, a
Monday, and the desk is open Monday to Friday only. There are 12 catalogue
items totalling 53 units, of which 40 are free right now, and 8 loans on the
register (L-1041 to L-1048).

Your mission is the money and the dates on the three-step new-loan flow:
record a borrower, put items and a schedule on the draft, review the figures,
and book it. You are not here to confirm that the happy path works — you are
here to find where the figures or the rules break down at the edges.

The desk's own rules are these, so you can tell a correct answer from a wrong
one:

- A pickup date must be a weekday, not in the past, and no more than 14
  calendar days after 2026-03-16 — so 2026-03-16 to 2026-03-30 inclusive.
- The loan period is 1, 3, 7 or 14 days. The due date is the pickup date plus
  that many calendar days; if it lands on a Saturday it moves on 2 days, if
  it lands on a Sunday it moves on 1 day. Kit is always due back at 17:00.
- The base charge is the sum of daily rate × quantity × days. If the loan
  carries 3 or more units in total, a bundle discount of 10% of the base
  charge is taken off. A deposit of 10% of replacement value is added per
  unit, but only for items whose replacement value is $1,000.00 or more.
  Percentages round to the nearest cent, halves up. The total due at pickup
  is base charge − bundle discount + deposit.
- A loan needs supervisor approval when its total replacement value is
  $2,500.00 or more, or when its period is 14 days; otherwise it goes
  straight to the pickup shelf.
- A loan can never commit more units of an item than are free.

Probe the boundaries those rules define — the first and last acceptable
pickup dates, the weekend either side of them, each of the four loan periods,
exactly 2 units versus exactly 3 units, an item priced just under and just
over the deposit threshold, a request just under and just over the approval
threshold, and asking for more units than are free. Try nonsense too:
negative or fractional quantities, an item listed twice, an unknown item, a
date that is not a date. Read the figures the desk quotes back and check the
arithmetic yourself. Where the desk accepts something it should refuse, or
quotes a number that does not follow its own rules, say so plainly.

### Story 13

You are testing Loanpoint, the equipment lending desk API used by Fairmont
University Media Services, before it goes live. One desk supervisor is signed
in; there is no login. The desk clock is frozen at Monday 16 March 2026, and
the desk is open Monday to Friday only. The register holds 8 loans:

- L-1041 Priya Raman, out, due back today 16 March.
- L-1042 Marcus Webb, out and overdue since 11 March, 3 units.
- L-1043 Dana Okoye, ready for pickup tomorrow 17 March, 2 units.
- L-1044 Theo Lindqvist, awaiting approval, pickup today, 14-day period.
- L-1045 Aisha Farouk, awaiting approval, pickup 18 March, 2 units.
- L-1046 Owen Castillo, returned back on 5 March.
- L-1047 Sofia Marchetti, cancelled on 9 March.
- L-1048 Nina Brandt, out, due back 19 March, 2 units.

Your mission is the life of a loan after it is booked — approving,
declining, marking picked up, extending, checking in, cancelling — and
whether the desk holds its own line when those are done in the wrong order,
twice over, or on the wrong loan.

The desk's rules are these:

- Only a loan that is ready for pickup can be marked picked up, and only on
  or after its pickup date.
- Only a loan that is out can be checked in. Checking in charges a late fee
  of $5.00 per unit for each business day after the due date up to today,
  capped at $150.00 per loan, and releases the deposit when the kit comes
  back in good condition or holds it when it is damaged or missing parts.
- Only a loan that is out, is not overdue, and has never been extended can be
  extended, once, by 7 days, free of charge.
- Only a loan awaiting approval or ready for pickup can be cancelled.
- Only a loan awaiting approval can be approved or declined, once.
- Units are committed while a loan is awaiting approval, ready or out, and go
  back into stock the moment it is cancelled, declined or returned. Available
  units are never below zero and never above the 53 units in the catalogue.
- The desk overview counters and the equipment availability figures must
  always agree with the loans behind them.

Attack the ordering: repeat an action that has already succeeded, act on a
loan in the wrong state, decline something already approved, extend the
overdue loan, check in something that never went out, cancel something
already returned, pick up a day early. After each action that does change
something, re-read the desk overview and the affected catalogue items and
check the counters and the stock still add up. Report anything the desk let
through that it should have refused, any figure that stopped agreeing with
the loans behind it, and any refusal whose wording would leave the supervisor
guessing.
